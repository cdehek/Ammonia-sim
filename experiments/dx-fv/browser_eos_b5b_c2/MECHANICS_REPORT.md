# 🔧 Ammonia Lab — The Mechanic’s Report

**CONDITIONALLY FEASIBLE**

The pressure gauge can look safely below 35 bar even when the saved mass,
energy and volume describe a mathematical state above it. Both original examples
still do exactly that in Chromium and desktop WebKit. All seven original
refusals still refuse. Nothing has been made easier to accept.

We found an important distinction in the earlier arithmetic: the saved inventory,
the rounded density/energy sent to the backend, and its rounded molar inputs
are different targets. At these tiny pressure differences that distinction can
change which side of the limit a calculation describes. We now keep all three
on the record. Both exact-inventory inward hazards remain outside in the
high-precision diagnostics.

A separate checking tool can put a defensible numerical fence around a local
liquid or vapor equation root and its pressure. That is real progress. But it
cannot yet prove that it picked the physically admissible phase. We deliberately
tested a wrong homogeneous branch: it can pass the local mathematics too.
Every complete certificate therefore remains **UNRESOLVED**.

The local checking step took about 15 milliseconds on this workstation, after
the root had already been located. Phase proof and browser certificate costs
are still unknown. Desktop WebKit says nothing about physical iPad performance.

The 35-bar limit and every original accuracy, conservation and solver rule stay
unchanged. The simulator, accepted A2, property adapters, EOS and C1 rollback
code are untouched. Mathematical pressure signs this small are not proof of a
real plant excursion or of ammonia measurements to that accuracy.

The next small research job should prove phase selection and saturation
inequalities before attempting browser integration. Gate B endpoints, Gate A
browser qualification, rejected-PT cleanup, full A2 qualification and iPad tests
remain separate unfinished jobs.
